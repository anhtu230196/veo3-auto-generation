## 1. A Fei — The 17-Day Walk in the Mist

In 2020, in a small, peaceful village called Long Wang in China, 39-year-old A Fei was living a quiet, unremarkable life.

One morning, she left her house to perform a familiar chore: walking down a long trail to the market to pick up some supplies. After finishing her shopping, she began her trek back home. However while on the road, she noticed something unusual—the fog was becoming significantly denser than she had ever seen before.

After walking for a while, near an area known as the "18 Lakes of Long Wang"—a spot popular with tourists—A Fei spotted a group of people standing on a nearby hill. Since it was a tourist destination, she assumed they were just visitors. But as she drew closer to strike up a conversation, they told her they were actually from Long Wang Village as well. They said they were just out for a stroll and warmly invited her to join them. A Fei agreed immediately, as she wanted to enjoy the fresh air.

Throughout the journey, A Fei felt the scenery was breathtakingly beautiful. Despite living there for years, she rarely went out, so she considered this a rare and special occasion. The group began to lead her deeper and deeper into the forest. They walked on and on until her body was completely exhausted. She barely had the strength to sit down on a large rock; before she could utter a single word, she lost consciousness right there.

A long period of time passed before A Fei finally came to. By then, it was pitch black. Although her mind was still hazy about the morning's events, her instincts told her she needed to get home immediately. In the silent darkness, she fumbled her way through the brush to find an exit. As soon as she reached the village, she cried out for help. Her parents rushed out, along with the police and other villagers; they were overjoyed that she had returned safely.

However, immediately following her return, a shocking detail emerged. When her parents asked where she had been for the past several days, A Fei innocently replied that she had only been gone for one night and had accidentally fallen asleep in the forest. She couldn't understand why everyone was making such a big deal out of it. It was then that her parents and the police told her the horrifying truth: She had been missing for exactly 17 days, not just one night. During that time, the entire village and the authorities had scoured every inch of the area looking for her, but to no avail.

A Fei began to describe the group of five people who had invited her for the walk; she even remembered each of their names vividly. When the people present heard those names, their blood ran cold. The people she described were indeed residents of the village, but they were all elders who had passed away many years ago. Not a single person on that list was still alive.

Even more chillingly, when police reviewed the security camera footage from the day she vanished, the video showed A Fei walking entirely alone that morning. She was seen walking, smiling, and talking to the empty air, with absolutely no one by her side.

## 2. Don Decker — The Rainmaker in the Cell

The second story takes place in 1983 and revolves around a man named Don Decker. At the time, Don was an inmate in prison. One day, the warden opened his cell and informed Don that he was being granted a furlough to attend his grandfather's funeral. After the service, Don didn't head straight back, but instead stopped by his friend Bob's house to ask if he could stay the night.

The strange events began in the bathroom. While washing his face, Don looked into the mirror and saw the image of an old man wearing a crown. Terrified, he collapsed to the floor, but when he looked back, the image was gone. Don assumed he was just hallucinating from exhaustion, so he hurried downstairs, sat in a chair, and told Bob what happened to try and calm down.

Right then, water began to leak from the ceiling. In just a few moments, the entire house was soaked. A panicked Bob called the landlord to investigate. They searched everywhere—from the kitchen to the upstairs bathroom—but the plumbing was perfectly fine. When they returned to the living room, Bob was stunned to see Don in a catatonic state, his eyes wide and staring blankly, completely unresponsive.

Bob decided to call the police. When the officers arrived, they were equally baffled, unable to explain where the water was coming from. That night, Bob's family stayed up all night cleaning, while Don remained seated in the same spot. Two days later, Don suddenly stood up, said nothing to anyone, silently opened the door, and walked out to head home. The moment his feet left the house, the water from the ceiling stopped instantly.

When Don returned to prison, his cellmate was terrified by Don's hollow expression and rushed to call the guards. Don then uttered a bizarre sentence: "I can make it rain." The guard thought Don was just talking nonsense, so he challenged him to try and make it rain inside the chief warden's office. Incredibly, inside that closed office, water began to manifest, soaking the chief's clothes from the inside out.

After several hours of chaos, a priest was called in. Seeing the soaked room, he looked directly at Don, pulled out a crucifix, and began to perform an exorcism. Don immediately began screaming violently, appearing to be in extreme pain before losing consciousness. When he woke up, he felt completely relieved.

Don later explained that on the day of the funeral, his grandfather's spirit had latched onto him. This grandfather had been a very cruel man in life, frequently abusing and mistreating Don when he was a child. It is possible that the evil spirit possessed Don and created that strange ability to manipulate water. After the exorcism, the water phenomenon never happened again. This story was publicly verified by many of the guards and police officers who witnessed it that day.

## 3. Carl Ledges and the 7-Year Secret Beneath the Well

In 1921, a farmer named Carl Ledges moved to the state of Ohio to find a plot of land for a ranch. He met the former owner of a property and completed the transaction. Before leaving, the previous owner told him something cryptic: "This area is very unlucky. Many people have failed here, which is why I wanted to sell the land." Carl was not a superstitious man, so he happily accepted and began building his horse ranch.

Life went on peacefully until one night around 9:30 PM. While checking the stables, Carl saw a person in the distance wearing a stark white outfit. He shouted out to ask who they were, but the figure didn't answer and instead walked silently toward the old well. Carl grabbed his gun for protection and gave chase, but as he reached the edge of the well, the figure vanished completely.

This phenomenon repeated every night following. It was always the same man in white, lingering near the well before disappearing. Carl began to believe this was truly a ghost, though it had not yet done him any harm.

Sometime later, while Carl and his staff were getting water for the horses, he noticed something strange: the horses refused to drink the water, even though they usually drank it without issue. Suspecting the water source was contaminated, Carl hired a cleaner to go down into the well. The worker used a pump to drain the water and then climbed down. Beneath a thick layer of mud, he struck something hard. When he dug it up, it was a shoe.

Carl ordered him to keep digging. This time, the worker was horrified to find human bones. The police were called and determined the remains belonged to a man named Henry. Further investigation revealed that Henry had been an employee of Frank—the ranch owner seven years prior. The two were known to argue loudly, often heard by neighbors, until one day Henry abruptly disappeared.

Thanks to the remains found in the well, police arrested Frank. Although he was only sentenced to seven months in prison due to a lack of direct evidence of the murder, the truth had finally come out. Most notably, when the remains were found, they also discovered Henry's pocket watch. The watch was stopped at exactly 9:35 PM—the precise time the white ghost always appeared to Carl. From the moment the remains were exhumed and given a proper burial, the ghost never appeared at the ranch again.

## 4. The Greenbrier Ghost and the Historic Trial

In 1896, in the Greenbrier area, there was a young couple named Zona and Edward Shue. They lived in a small house where Edward worked as a blacksmith and Zona stayed home as a housewife. On January 23, an 11-year-old boy—Edward's assistant—was sent by Edward to the house to ask if Zona needed anything from the store.

When the boy entered, he found Zona lying motionless at the base of the stairs. Terrified, he ran home to tell Edward. A doctor arrived and found that Edward had already moved Zona to the bed. He was sitting beside her, weeping uncontrollably. When the doctor tried to examine the body, Edward became extremely agitated and obstructive. Ultimately, the doctor made a cursory conclusion that she died of heart failure.

Before the burial, Edward took out a scarf—Zona's favorite—and wrapped it tightly around her neck, saying he wanted her to look her best when she was gone. During the funeral, Mary Jane—Zona's mother—though deeply grieved, watched her son-in-law with growing suspicion.

After the funeral, Mary Jane prayed constantly to see her daughter and find out what had really happened. Many days passed until one night, the candles in her room suddenly went out. A light appeared, and Zona's spirit manifested in a black dress. When her mother asked why she had died, Zona replied simply: "It was Edward." Then, the ghost opened her mouth as if struggling for air, turned her head a full 180 degrees, and vanished.

Mary Jane immediately went to the prosecutor and told the whole story. As absurd as it sounded, her sincerity convinced the prosecutor to order an exhumation. Only then did the doctor perform a thorough examination, discovering that beneath the scarf were clear ligature marks; Zona's neck had been broken.

Edward eventually confessed that he had murdered his wife in a fit of rage and then pushed her down the stairs to stage an accident. He had wrapped the scarf around her neck specifically to hide the strangulation marks. Edward was sentenced to life in prison and later died there of an unknown illness. At the cemetery where Zona was laid to rest, a plaque was erected telling the story of the "Greenbrier Ghost"—the spirit who returned to claim justice for her own death.

## 5. Chu Xiu-hua — The Reincarnation Case That Shocked Taiwan

Taiwan, the 1950s. In a remote countryside village, a woman named Lin Wang-lu suddenly fell critically ill. She had always been physically frail. Miles away, her husband, Wu Xiong-ming, received the terrifying news. Panicked, he jumped on his bicycle and started pedaling as fast as he could, desperate to see his wife one last time.

On his way home, something incredibly bizarre happened. Wu suddenly felt his bike grow unnaturally heavy. The tires flattened out, acting like a full-grown adult had just hopped onto the back seat. Even so, he gritted his teeth and poured every ounce of his energy into pedaling, his heart racing with anxiety. But it was too late. By the time he walked through the door, his wife had already taken her last breath.

The house immediately filled with the heavy sounds of grief. But as Wu approached the coffin to look at his wife one final time, a terrifying scene unfolded right before everyone's eyes. Lin suddenly moved, slowly sat up, and started looking around the room. People screamed in pure terror, and some even ran out of the house.

After resting and recovering, Lin was actually back to life in the flesh. The catch? The body belonged to Lin, but the soul inside was someone else entirely.

Her personality flipped 180 degrees. This previously illiterate country woman suddenly loved reading books and newspapers. But the detail that completely shell-shocked her family and neighbors was that she suddenly started speaking fluent Mandarin.

Here is why that is scientifically impossible: Back in the 1950s, rural Taiwanese locals only spoke their native Taiwanese dialect. Mandarin—the standard Chinese spoken today—was brought over from the mainland and was strictly used by the government and schools in major cities. For an illiterate woman who had never even left her village to wake up from the dead speaking this "foreign language" with flawless pronunciation made absolutely no sense.

Naturally, this wild story caught the attention of the Taiwanese government. They took her to a military hospital for a full psychiatric and physical evaluation. The moment she walked into the clinic and saw the chief military doctor, she burst into tears, rushed over to grab his hands, and called out, "Shūshu!"—which means "Uncle."

She claimed her real name was Chu Xiu-hua, a 17-year-old girl from Kinmen Island. It turned out she was actually the doctor's long-lost niece who had vanished years ago.

Chu Xiu-hua explained what happened: In 1958, war broke out on Kinmen Island, so she and a group of locals boarded a refugee boat to escape. After drifting at sea for days, a group of Taiwanese fishermen found them. But instead of saving them, these greedy men robbed the families of all their gold and valuables, then callously threw everyone into the deep ocean to erase the evidence.

To prove she was telling the truth, Chu Xiu-hua—acting through Lin's body—personally led government investigators to the exact stretch of sea where she was attacked. She pointed out the exact fishing boat and the men who did it.

When the police raided the vessel, a surviving fisherman dropped to his knees, trembling, and confessed to everything. His confession matched her story 100%. He even accurately described a young girl named Chu Xiu-hua who had begged for her life before being thrown into the freezing water.

All doubts were instantly cleared. People came to believe that on that fateful day, Chu's restless soul drifted ashore and hitched a ride on the back of Wu's bicycle to follow him home. The exact second Lin drew her last breath, leaving her physical body empty, Chu's soul claimed it to come back to life and bring a brutal mass murder to light.

Chu ended up living inside Lin's body for the next 40 years, dedicating her life to charity work at a local temple, and passed away at the age of 97. On the day of her funeral, out of pure respect for this miraculous and mysterious event, her family engraved both names onto her ancestral tablet: Lin Wang-lu and Chu Xiu-hua.

## 6. Marico Iguchi and the Killer's Haunting

Kaohsiung, Taiwan. 1991. At a local taxi lot, a group of drivers stood chatting when they noticed something highly unusual. A young driver named Liu Jie-ming ran past in a massive rush, locking himself straight inside his cab.

His car looked incredibly bizarre. It was plastered completely with protective spiritual talismans, and several Buddha statues were lined up right on the dashboard. Liu's face was twisted in pure panic and extreme anxiety.

In the company, Liu was a complete loner who never started a conversation. Whenever someone did talk to him, he was aggressive and irritable, causing his coworkers to stay away. He also had a deep hatred for stray animals. Whenever they annoyed him, he would pull out a crossbow he kept in his car and use it on them.

Liu's daily routine completely changed after that. He practically stayed barricaded inside his taxi from dawn to dusk. Although he owned a private apartment, he almost never went home. On rare occasions, he would run inside just to grab his things or use the restroom, and then he would immediately bolt back out to his car.

One day, the moment Liu stepped out of his apartment building, several police cruisers suddenly surrounded him, taking him into custody. The police were investigating the mysterious disappearance of a Japanese tourist named Marico Iguchi.

Marico had traveled to Taiwan alone and frequently used taxis to get around. The moment she arrived in the city of Kaohsiung, she vanished without a trace. Authorities put many local taxi drivers under surveillance, and Liu quickly became their number-one suspect. His bizarre behavior—living inside his vehicle surrounded by talismans and statues—had been secretly tracked by undercover cops for days before they decided to close in.

Under interrogation, Liu broke down and confessed to taking Marico's life. That night, Marico had hailed Liu's cab. She mentioned she hadn't found a place to stay yet and asked him to drive her to a hotel. Seeing a foreign woman traveling alone, Liu faked an act of kindness. He suggested she stay over at his apartment—a fairly common lodging setup for travelers back then. Trusting his apparent generosity, Marico agreed.

However, once inside the room, Liu's true intentions came to light. He demanded unwanted physical closeness, but Marico flatly rejected him. The next morning, taking advantage of the moment she was fast asleep, Liu used his crossbow to end her life. To conceal the evidence, he separated the remains into smaller parts, placed everything into large bags, loaded them into his car, and drove to a remote area to hide them away. He threw the crossbow away at the exact same location.

Liu recounted a terrifying detail. On the very first night after committing the act, a horrific event occurred. While sleeping in his apartment, a strange voice jolted him awake.

The moment he opened his eyes, he was paralyzed with fear. Floating right in the middle of the room was a terrifying visage of her face covered in dark crimson stains. Liu bolted in pure panic, but the floating phantom chased him ruthlessly, repeatedly demanding, "Why?"

Since that fateful night, the apartment became Liu's worst nightmare. He never dared to set foot inside his home again and moved permanently into his taxi, relying on the talismans and statues in a desperate attempt to ward off Marico's spirit.

Liu was later sentenced to life in prison. Law enforcement escorted him back to the disposal site to retrieve the remains of the victim. They managed to find almost every hidden part, except for her upper remains—which to this day, have never been found.

The case was officially closed, but the paranormal occurrences surrounding it continue to haunt the people of Taiwan. Before Liu was caught, multiple households reported receiving mysterious phone calls where a woman's voice speaking Japanese would echo through the line before abruptly hanging up.

Furthermore, a police officer who participated in inspecting the apartment crime scene firmly claimed that he felt an invisible force give him a violent shove from behind. But when he turned around, there was absolutely nobody there.
